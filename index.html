Topic 3.1: Common Synthesizer Waveforms

Each waveform shape has a different mix of harmonics, and that mix gives it its color. Select a waveform to inspect its oscilloscope shape (Figures 3–7) and listen to its raw harmonic timbre.

Listening to: Sine wave (purest tone, only fundamental).

Figure 3: Sine Wave

This is the purest sound, with no extra harmonics. Only the fundamental is present, which is why it sounds pure and simple.

It sounds like a tuning fork or the soft tone of a flute.
Harmonic note: Harmonics are numbered 1st, 2nd, 3rd and so on. The odd-numbered ones (1st, 3rd, 5th) give a hollow sound. The even-numbered ones (2nd, 4th, 6th) add fullness. Square and triangle waves use only odd harmonics (the triangle’s fall off much faster). Saw waves use both odd and even. A pulse wave at 25% is missing every fourth harmonic.
